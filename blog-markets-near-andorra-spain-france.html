Markets worth the drive from Andorra: the best in Spain and France

By Rachel, 30 October 2026

Andorra's shops are excellent, but for a proper market, with growers, cheesemakers and striped awnings, most of us cross a border. Within an hour and a half of Andorra la Vella are some of the best markets in the Pyrenees, in Spain and in France. Here's where to go on which day, what to buy, and the fairs worth planning a weekend around. More on the neighbours in Andorra and France.

At a glance

  • The nearest is the oldest: La Seu d'Urgell, about 20 minutes into Spain, holds its market on Tuesdays and Saturdays and has done since at least 1029.
  • For a Sunday, choose Puigcerdà in Spain, or Saillagouse or Les Cabannes in France.
  • Saturday is the busiest day, with six markets in range.
  • Go early. Most markets pack up between 1pm and 2pm.
  • The big fair this autumn is in Puigcerdà on 7 and 8 November 2026.

Spain

La Seu d’Urgell

Tuesday and Saturday morningsAbout 20 minutes from Andorra la Vella

This is the one to start with. La Seu's market has been documented since 1029, and nearly 70 stalls fill the old town on Tuesdays and Saturdays: along the arcades of Carrer Major, in Plaça dels Oms and in Plaça Patalín. About half sell food, with fruit and vegetables from local gardens, honey, cured sausage and cheese; the rest sell clothes, shoes and crafts. Stallholders set up from about 8.30am and stay until 2pm, a little less in winter.

What to buy: cheese and butter. The Alt Urgell and Cerdanya have their own protected cheese and butter, both made from the milk of the two valleys.

On Saturdays, two more markets join in. The Encants dels Canonges, a second hand market run by local people, fills the south side of Carrer dels Canonges every Saturday, and on the first Saturday of each month La Seu Antic, an antiques and collectors' fair, sets up on Passeig Pasqual Ingla and Plaça Joan Sansa.

Make a morning of it: the Romanesque cathedral of Santa Maria is a few steps from the stalls.

Bellver de Cerdanya

Thursday mornings, 8am to 1pmAbout 50 minutes from Andorra la Vella

A small market where people from the valley do their weekly shop, rather than a day out in itself. Turisme Cerdanya lists it every Thursday from 8am to 1pm. Come for local cheese, Cerdanya sausage and honey, and pair it with lunch in the hilltop old town. Bellver's big day is the Fira de Sant Llorenç on 10 August, with a market of artisan food from across the Pyrenees.

Llívia

Saturday mornings, 8am to 1pmAbout 1 hour 10 from Andorra la Vella

Llívia is a Spanish town entirely surrounded by France, and its Saturday market, listed by Turisme Cerdanya from 8am to 1pm, is small but a little different, with French cheeses alongside Catalan charcuterie. Alp, near La Molina, holds its own Saturday market at the same time.

Puigcerdà

Sunday mornings, 8am to 2pmAbout 1 hour 10 from Andorra la Vella

The Cerdanya's capital holds its market every Sunday on Passeig 10 d'Abril, from 8am to 2pm according to Turisme Cerdanya. Expect goat's cheese, honey, herbs, wild mushrooms in season, and baskets. Afterwards, walk round the lake at the top of the town and stay for lunch. Saillagouse's Sunday market is a short drive over the French border, so you can do both.

Getting there: the quickest way is usually through La Seu d'Urgell and along the valley, but you can also come over the Port d'Envalira and through France.

France

Ax-les-Thermes, Les Cabannes, Tarascon and Foix are reached over the Port d'Envalira, or through the Envalira tunnel, which has a toll. Bourg-Madame and Saillagouse sit next to Puigcerdà, so you can go either way. In winter, check the road before you leave and carry chains or fit winter tyres.

Ax-les-Thermes

Tuesday and Saturday mornings, 7am to 1pmAbout 1 hour from Andorra la Vella

The first sizeable town on the road down from Pas de la Casa. Its market runs on Tuesdays and Saturdays all year, in the centre by the town hall, and on Thursdays as well in high summer. Ax is a spa town, so finish by sitting with your feet in the Bassin des Ladres, the free hot spring foot bath in the middle of town.

Bourg-Madame

Saturday morningsAbout 1 hour 10 from Andorra la Vella

Bourg-Madame sits across the river from Puigcerdà, and its Saturday market is a general French market: fruit and vegetables, meat, fish, and some clothes and household goods. Combine it with Llívia's Saturday market, a short drive away. Bourg-Madame also holds a foire au gras, a fair for foie gras, duck and goose, in early November.

Les Cabannes

Sunday mornings, 8am to 1pmAbout 1 hour 10 from Andorra la Vella

A village market in the Ariège valley, between Ax and Tarascon, held every Sunday on Place des Platanes all year: fruit and vegetables, dairy, meat and local crafts. It's a good reason for a Sunday drive into France, when many shops there are closed.

Saillagouse

Sunday morningsAbout 1 hour 15 from Andorra la Vella

A producers' market in front of the town hall, so most of what's on sale was grown or made by the person selling it. It's a short drive from Puigcerdà, so you can do both on the same Sunday. Saillagouse's Christmas market, with a wine fair, is usually on the first weekend of December.

Tarascon-sur-Ariège

Wednesday and Saturday mornings, 8am to 1pmAbout 1 hour 20 from Andorra la Vella

Two markets a week: on Place Jean Jaurès on Wednesdays and on Place du 19 Mars 1962 on Saturdays, both with produce, dairy, meat and local crafts. Tarascon's Christmas market is on Saturday 12 December 2026.

Worth the extra few minutes: Foix

Friday mornings, 8am to 1pmAbout 1 hour 35 from Andorra la Vella

Just outside our 90 minutes, but Foix's big Friday market is worth it. It spreads through the town below the castle: poultry, charcuterie, fish and cheese in the Halle aux Grains, fruit and vegetables in the Halle Saint-Volusien, and everything else along the Allées de Villote. There's a smaller market on Tuesdays.

Fairs worth planning a weekend around

  • Fira de Puigcerdà, 7 and 8 November 2026. The town's big autumn fair, famous for its horse show of the Pyrenean breed. In 2024 it had more than 300 stalls of cheese, charcuterie, crafts and clothes. In 2025 the horse show was cancelled for animal health reasons, and the fair went ahead in the town centre with more than 230 stalls, so check whether the horses are back this year.
  • Bourg-Madame's foire au gras, early November.
  • La Seu d'Urgell's Christmas market, 6 to 8 December. Craft stalls, food and music on Passeig Joan Brudieu.
  • Saillagouse's Christmas market and wine fair, usually the first weekend of December.
  • Tarascon-sur-Ariège's Christmas market, Saturday 12 December 2026.
  • Fira de Sant Ermengol, La Seu d'Urgell, every October. The Pyrenees' artisan cheese fair, with tastings and a market. The 2026 edition, on 16 to 18 October, brought together 48 cheesemakers.
  • In summer: Bellver's Fira de Sant Llorenç on 10 August, and a producers' market in Bolvir, which ran every Saturday morning in August 2026.

For Andorra's own Christmas village and market, see Christmas in Andorra.

Before you go

  • Go early. The best produce goes first, and most stalls pack up by 1pm.
  • Bring cash and a cool bag. Smaller stalls may not take cards, and cheese travels better cold.
  • Take your passport or ID card. Andorra is outside the EU, so expect checks at the border.
  • Mind the allowance coming home. Andorra lets each person bring in up to €900 of goods without paying Andorra's sales tax, IGI, or €300 if you've reclaimed the Spanish or French VAT. Above that, declare it at the border and pay the IGI. A market shop rarely comes close.
  • Allow for the border. Queues build at weekends and at the end of holidays, especially coming back into Andorra.
