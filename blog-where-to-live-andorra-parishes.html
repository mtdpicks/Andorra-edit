Where to live in Andorra: the seven parishes compared, with a map

By Rachel, 25 November 2026

Andorra is small enough to cross in under an hour, but its seven parishes feel very different to live in. Some are busy valley towns with everything on the doorstep, others are quiet stone villages or ski villages high up the valleys. Here's a map of all seven, with what homes cost in each, and an honest guide to who each parish suits.

Andorra la Vella

The capital, at 1,023 m, is the most practical place to live. The Government, the Consell General, most offices and banks, the main shopping streets and the national bus station are all here, so you can manage perfectly well without a car. The French lycée, the Lycée Comte de Foix, is in town, and the British College of Andorra is in the hills above it, about ten minutes from the centre.

  • Why live here. Everything within walking distance, the coaches to Barcelona and Toulouse airports, and the most choice of flats to rent and buy.
  • Think about. Traffic and noise in the centre, and prices to match, at about €7,726 per m². Santa Coloma, in the south of the parish, was much cheaper, at about €5,467.
  • Nursery. €512 a month for a full day, or about €384 for parish residents.

See our Andorra la Vella guide and buses in Andorra.

Escaldes-Engordany

Escaldes runs straight on from the capital, and the two feel like one town. It's the youngest parish, created in 1978, and the most sought after: nearly a third of the flats listed for sale in Q2 2026 were here, at the highest average asking price, about €9,552 per m². Its hot springs feed Caldea and the hotel spas, the country's public hospital, the Hospital Nostra Senyora de Meritxell, is here, and the Madriu valley, Andorra's World Heritage site, starts just above the town.

  • Why live here. Smart flats, many of the best shops and restaurants, the hospital close by, and walks into the mountains from your door.
  • Think about. The highest prices and rents in Andorra, with asking rents averaging about €3,799 a month.
  • Nursery. The cheapest in the country, at €358 a month for a full day, and the same for everyone.

See our Escaldes-Engordany guide and Caldea, honestly.

Encamp

Encamp is where many Andorrans live, an everyday town at about 1,230 m, ten minutes from the capital on the road to France. It was the cheapest parish to buy in, at about €5,840 per m², and the cheapest to rent, at about €1,328 a month, and the Funicamp gondola lifts you from the town into Grandvalira in about 15 minutes. The parish runs all the way to the French border at Pas de la Casa, where flats averaged about €4,400 per m², the lowest figure in the report.

  • Why live here. The best value in the central valley, a real town with shops and schools, and skiing without moving to a ski village.
  • Think about. It's busier and less pretty than the villages higher up, and Pas de la Casa is very much a resort town.
  • Nursery. €397.30 a month for a full day.

See our Encamp guide and Pas de la Casa.

La Massana

La Massana sits at about 1,250 m, 7 km from the capital, where the road divides for Arinsal and Ordino. It's greener and quieter than the central valley and popular with families: Agora, an international school that teaches in English and Spanish, is at L'Aldosa, the gondola from the town climbs to Pal Arinsal, and the Comapedrosa Natural Park surrounds Andorra's highest peak. Flats averaged about €6,704 per m², from about €5,697 in Arinsal to €9,167 in Escàs.

  • Why live here. Space, mountain views, an international school, and skiing and mountain biking from the town.
  • Think about. It's a little cooler than Andorra la Vella, and the prettiest villages, such as Escàs, cost nearly as much as Escaldes.
  • Nursery. €410 a month for a full day.

See our La Massana guide and Pal Arinsal.

Sant Julià de Lòria

Sant Julià is the first town from Spain, about 15 minutes south of the capital, and the lowest parish, dropping to 840 m where the Runer meets the Valira. Its lower, sunnier slopes are where most of Andorra's wine is made, and it's home to the University of Andorra and to Naturland. Asking prices averaged about €5,946 per m², though very few flats were listed, and rents about €1,650 a month.

  • Why live here. Milder weather, good value, and the quickest drive to La Seu d'Urgell and the rest of Spain.
  • Think about. It's a working town rather than a pretty one, and the ski areas are a longer drive away.
  • Nursery. €415.33 a month for a full day.

See our Sant Julià de Lòria guide and Andorran wine.

Canillo

Canillo is the largest parish by area and the fastest growing, up 5% in the year to July 2026. The village sits at about 1,500 m, twenty minutes from the capital, and the parish runs up the valley to El Tarter and Soldeu, so Grandvalira is on the doorstep all winter. Prices vary more than anywhere else: about €9,901 per m² in El Vilar, the highest in the report, against €5,610 in Soldeu and €5,093 in Ransol. Asking rents averaged about €2,539 a month.

  • Why live here. Skiing from home, space, and the walks and lakes of the high valleys in summer.
  • Think about. Longer, snowier winters, a drive for most shopping, and quiet ski villages out of season.
  • Nursery. €387.70 a month for a full day.

See our Canillo guide and Grandvalira.

Ordino

Ordino has the fewest residents of the seven, 5,816, in stone villages along a single valley road at about 1,300 m, 9 km north of the capital. It's the parish for peace and nature: the Sorteny meadows fill with wild flowers in early summer, and Ordino Arcalís, at the top of the valley, is usually the first ski area in Andorra to open. Flats averaged about €7,675 per m², and about €9,198 in El Serrat, with asking rents around €1,988 a month.

  • Why live here. Village life, clean air and some of the most beautiful scenery in the country, with the capital still close.
  • Think about. Few shops and little choice of homes, and a drive or bus for almost everything.
  • Nursery. The dearest, at €440.13 a month for residents and €490.59 for everyone else.

See our Ordino guide and Ordino Arcalís.

How to choose

  • No car. Andorra la Vella or Escaldes-Engordany, where you can walk to almost everything.
  • Skiing every day. Canillo, or Encamp for better value with the Funicamp; La Massana for Pal Arinsal.
  • Children. La Massana for Agora, or Andorra la Vella and Escaldes-Engordany for the British College; the free Andorran, French and Spanish systems have schools in every parish. See schools in Andorra.
  • Value. Encamp and Sant Julià de Lòria were the cheapest parishes to buy in.
  • Peace and quiet. Ordino, or the villages of La Massana and Canillo.
  • Sun and Spain. Sant Julià de Lòria, the lowest and southernmost parish.

Wherever you live

Homes are scarce everywhere, so start looking early, and expect the parish figures to swing from one quarter to the next with so few listings. Every buyer pays the 4% property transfer tax, 1% to the Government and 3% to the parish; a copy of every rental contract goes to your parish's comú, and you'll need proof of a home when you apply for residency. The national buses link all seven parishes, and even the furthest village is well under an hour's drive from the capital.
