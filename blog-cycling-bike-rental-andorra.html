Cycling in Andorra: bike hire, prices, rules and the routes worth riding

By Rachel, 10 October 2026

Andorra is one of Europe's great cycling playgrounds: Tour de France climbs straight out of town, lift served mountain bike trails, and e-bike circuits that let the gondolas do the hard work. Here's how to hire a bike, what it costs and where to ride.

Which bike to hire

Hire shops stock everything from carbon road bikes to junior e-bikes. Andorra's largest chain, Viladomat, includes a helmet with every rental, and adds a saddle bag with a spare tube, tyre levers, pump and multi tool for road, gravel and e-bikes.

  • Road bike

    Carbon road bikes from brands such as Scott, BMC and Colnago, for the big cols.

  • Gravel bike

    Wider tyres for mixing tarmac with forest tracks.

  • E-bike

    Batteries of up to 800 Wh give roughly 80 to 120 km of range on Andorra's climbs. From about €75 a day.

  • Mountain bike

    Cross country, enduro and downhill bikes, ready for the Bike Park.

  • Kids' bikes

    Junior e-bikes, enduro and 24 inch bikes, with a full face helmet included.

The routes worth riding

From the Tour de France's favourite summit finishes to a gentle family ride along the iron route.

  • 2,408 m

    Port d'Envalira

    The highest paved road in the Pyrenees. From Pas de la Casa it is a short, sharp 4.9 km at 6.4%, with France at your back and the whole of Grandvalira ahead.

    View the route on Komoot

  • 18.3 km at 5.2%

    Ordino Arcalís

    Tour de France territory: Jan Ullrich took the yellow jersey here in 1997, and it finished stage 9 in 2016. Up the Ordino valley to 2,229 m, with ramps of 12% near El Serrat.

  • 8.3–8.7% average

    Coll de la Gallina

    Andorra's hardest climb, from Aixovall or Sant Julià de Lòria to 1,910 m. Steep, quiet and with huge valley views.

    View the route on Komoot

  • 8.9 km at 5.1%

    Coll d'Ordino

    From Canillo, the gentlest of the classic cols, through pine forest to the pass above Ordino. A perfect first climb.

    View the route on Komoot

  • 4.2 km

    Ruta del Ferro

    A relaxed, mostly flat ride past old coal yards and forges from Andorra's iron age. Ideal with children or on an e-bike.

    View the route on AllTrails

  • 60+ km of trails

    Pal Arinsal Bike Park

    Downhill, enduro, beginner and family trails served by lifts. It hosts the UCI MTB World Series in July 2026.

  • 102 km

    Grandvalira e-bike circuits

    Signposted e-bike circuits from Soldeu, Canillo and the Funicamp in Encamp, with the gondolas doing the climbing and five charging points.

Rules of the road

  • Wear a helmet. It comes with every hire, and a full face helmet is required in the Bike Park.
  • Give and expect space. Andorra's government has campaigned for drivers to leave at least 1.5 m when overtaking cyclists. Ride on the right and in single file on busy valley roads.
  • Mind the traffic. The main valley roads carry heavy traffic, especially at weekends and on the way to the border, so set off early.
  • Dress for the descent. At over 2,000 m it can be cold and windy even in July. Pack a gilet and gloves for the way down.
  • Carry ID. The big climbs lead to the French and Spanish borders, so take your passport or ID card.

When to go

The bike season runs from May to October, with June and September usually the least crowded. The Bike Park and the lifts open in summer; check each resort's dates before you travel. Summer in Andorra.
