Andorra la Vella

At 1,023 m, Andorra la Vella is the highest capital in Europe, and it has two sides: a stone old quarter around a parliament house from 1580, and a shopping street that runs on for more than a kilometre into Escaldes.

Andorra la Vella at a glance

Andorra la Vella sits in the valley of the Gran Valira at 1,023 m, merging seamlessly with Escaldes-Engordany next door. It's the country's political centre and its transport hub, and the easiest base for a first visit.

The parish also takes in the old village of Santa Coloma to the south, and rises to the forests and pastures above the town.

Where Andorra la Vella is

Andorra la Vella sits in the central valley, just downstream from Escaldes-Engordany, where the Valira d’Orient and the Valira del Nord meet. La Seu d’Urgell, the first town in Spain, is about 20 minutes’ drive south, and Barcelona and Toulouse are each about three hours away. Its name means ‘Andorra the Old’. Where is Andorra?

Weather in Andorra la Vella

At 1,023 m, the capital averages about 4.4°C in January and 20.8°C in August, and most months have 20 to 25 days without rain or snow. Winter nights average just below freezing in January and February, and summer afternoons reach the high 20s. The weather by month.

Things to do in Andorra la Vella

Casa de la Vall

A stone house from 1580 that was the seat of the Consell General until 2011. Inside, you'll see the history of the co-principality, the flag and coat of arms, and the cabinet of the seven keys, with one lock for each parish. It reopened on 23 December 2025 after an interior renovation; tickets cost €5.

The Barri Antic

The old quarter's lanes climb around Casa de la Vall and the Romanesque church of Sant Esteve, one of the country's most historic. In the evenings, the bars around Plaça Guillemó and the old quarter are the liveliest in town.

Dalí and Plensa

Salvador Dalí's bronze La Noblesse du Temps, a melting clock draped over a tree trunk, weighs about 1,400 kg and has stood by Plaça de la Rotonda since 2010. Below the new parliament, Jaume Plensa's Seven Poets sit on tall poles and glow at night.

Plaça del Poble

A 16,500 m² square built on the roof of the government building, with views across the valley. It hosts fairs and festivities, and the Christmas village in winter. Christmas in Andorra.

The Rec del Solà

An easy, paved 4 km return walk along an irrigation canal built in 1880, with a large viewpoint over the capital. It's the best way to see the town from above.

Santa Coloma

One of Andorra's oldest villages, at the south of the parish, known for its church with a rare round bell tower.

Parc Central

The capital's biggest green space, 33,000 m² by the river, with play areas and water features. A good stop with children.

Light and water

After dark, the fountains along the Passeig de la Rotonda put on a show of light, water and music, with times changing through the seasons.

La Margineda bridge

Andorra’s most iconic medieval bridge spans the Valira at La Margineda, on the old road south to Sant Julià de Lòria. It is 33 m long and dates from around 1400, and a sculpture by Andreu Alfaro stands beside it.

The Festa Major

The capital’s summer festival fills the town with concerts and family events. In 2026 it ran from 30 July to 3 August, with 67 events, from the opening concerts in Plaça Guillemó to havaneres singing.

Half a day in the capital

Start at Plaça de la Rotonda with Dalí’s melting clock, then walk along Avinguda Meritxell to the old quarter. See Casa de la Vall and Sant Esteve, and stop for lunch around Plaça Guillemó. Take in the view from Plaça del Poble or climb to the Rec del Solà path, then finish with the fountain show by the Rotonda after dark, or an evening at Caldea.

Avinguda Meritxell

The capital's main shopping street runs through the town and on into Escaldes-Engordany, lined with perfume, skincare, fashion and electronics shops and the Pyrénées department store. The Super U supermarket is inside the CCA centre at number 92. Shopping in Andorra.

Where to eat

Celler d'en Toni, run by the same family since 1964, is listed in the Michelin Guide, and Can Manel holds the Traditional Andorran Cuisine seal. For a fireside meal, Borda Estevet is just above town on the Carretera de la Comella. Restaurants by cuisine.

Where to stay

Staying in the capital puts Caldea, the shops and the bus station on your doorstep. The Grand Plaza and Suites Plaza Hotel & Wellness is central and a short walk from Caldea. Where to stay in Andorra.

The bus hub

Every national bus line meets at the bus station in Andorra la Vella, with routes to all the parishes and ski villages, and direct coaches to Barcelona and Toulouse. Getting to Andorra.

Skiing from Andorra la Vella

There’s no skiing in the capital itself, but it makes a practical base, with more choice of hotels and restaurants, and Caldea for the evenings.

Grandvalira’s Funicamp gondola starts in Encamp, about 10 minutes’ drive away, and climbs 6 km to the slopes in around 15 minutes. National bus line L2 runs to Encamp every 20 minutes on weekdays and every 30 at weekends. For Pal Arinsal, a cable car rises from La Massana. See Grandvalira, Pal Arinsal and ski hire.

Caldea, just next door

Escaldes-Engordany begins where Andorra la Vella ends, and Caldea's glass spire rises over the river between them. Spend an afternoon in the thermal lagoons after a morning in the old town. Caldea, honestly.

Questions people ask

What is there to do in Andorra la Vella?

Visit Casa de la Vall, the 16th century former parliament, and the old quarter around it; see Dalí's Nobility of Time and Jaume Plensa's Seven Poets; walk the Rec del Solà irrigation canal path for views; and shop along Avinguda Meritxell. Caldea's thermal spa is next door in Escaldes-Engordany.

Is Andorra la Vella worth visiting?

Yes. It is Europe's highest capital, at 1,023 m, with a compact old quarter, good restaurants, tax free shopping, and the national bus station that connects every parish and ski resort.

How do you get to Andorra la Vella from Barcelona?

Direct coaches run from Barcelona airport and the city to the national bus station in Andorra la Vella, taking about three hours. By car it is about 200 km.

Can you ski from Andorra la Vella?

Not from the town itself, but the slopes are close. Buses run from the capital to every resort, the Funicamp gondola in Encamp rises into Grandvalira, and a cable car from La Massana reaches Pal Arinsal.

What is the capital of Andorra?

Andorra la Vella. At 1,023 m it's the highest capital in Europe, and its name means ‘Andorra the Old’.

Where is Andorra la Vella?

In Andorra's central valley, in the Pyrenees between France and Spain. La Seu d'Urgell in Spain is about 20 minutes' drive away, and Barcelona and Toulouse about three hours.

When is the Festa Major in Andorra la Vella?

In summer. In 2026 it ran from 30 July to 3 August, with 67 events for all ages.
