Retiring to Andorra: residence, tax, healthcare and the 2026 rules

By Rachel, 31 October 2026

Clean mountain air, low taxes, a safe small country and Spain and France on the doorstep: it's easy to see why people retire to Andorra. But the route has narrowed. Since February 2026, living here without working needs a €1 million investment, permits are limited by an annual quota, and you must spend at least 90 days a year here. Here's what retiring to Andorra involves now, including how the new UK tax treaty treats your pension.

At a glance

  • The route: residence without gainful activity, often called passive residence.
  • The cost: a €1 million investment, plus a €50,000 payment to the Andorran Financial Authority that isn't refunded.
  • The quota: 163 permits for 2026, and some earlier applicants were refused when the previous quota ran out.
  • Time here: at least 90 days a year.
  • Tax: income tax is capped at 10%, and the first €24,000 is tax free.
  • UK pensions: a UK and Andorra tax treaty has applied since 2026, so most pensions are taxed only where you live.

The residence route for retirees

There's no retirement visa as such. Most people who retire here apply for residence without gainful activity, which lets you live in Andorra as long as you don't work here.

The quota matters. In March 2026, about 60 applications were refused because the quota ran out while they were being processed, and some had been filed in late 2024. Apply early in the year, with every document ready, and use an Andorran lawyer or gestoria who handles residence applications every week.

The 90 day rule

Passive residents must spend at least 90 days a year in Andorra. The rule, already in Andorran law, is also part of Andorra's agreement with the EU on the new Entry/Exit System, to make sure residence reflects real ties to the country, and checks on actual presence are expected to increase. Keep records of the time you spend here.

Tax and your pension

Andorra's income tax is low and simple. According to the Government's income tax guide, the first €24,000 of income is tax free, income between €24,000 and €40,000 is taxed at an effective 5%, and anything above at 10%. The tax free allowance can rise to €40,000 if your spouse or partner lives with you and has no income of their own.

There's no inheritance tax and no wealth tax, and no gift tax for individual residents.

The UK and Andorra tax treaty

For British retirees, the big change is the UK and Andorra double taxation convention. It was signed in February 2025 and came into force on 22 December 2025, taking effect in Andorra from 1 January 2026 and for UK income tax from 6 April 2026. Under the treaty:

  • Pensions paid to a resident of one country are taxable only in that country (Article 17). If you're tax resident in Andorra, that generally means Andorra.
  • Government service pensions, for example from the civil service, are taxable only in the UK, unless you're both resident in Andorra and an Andorran national (Article 18).

Living here as a passive resident doesn't automatically make you tax resident here. That depends on where you spend your time and where your main interests are, so take advice from a tax adviser who knows both countries before you move.

Your UK State Pension

You can have your UK State Pension paid while you live abroad, and you claim it through the International Pension Centre within four months of reaching State Pension age. Yearly increases aren't paid in every country, so ask the International Pension Centre whether yours will go up each year while you live in Andorra.

Healthcare

Passive residents are expected to hold private health insurance. Residents who work pay into CASS, Andorra's social security fund. Andorra's public hospital is the Hospital Nostra Senyora de Meritxell in Escaldes-Engordany, and there's a geriatric clinic, Sant Vicenç d'Enclar, in Santa Coloma.

The hospital is growing. In 2026 the Consell General approved just over €5 million for upgrades including its first MRI scanner and robotic surgery, added a fifth operating theatre, and is extending hospital care at home to elderly care residences. Some specialist treatment is still referred to hospitals in Barcelona. See pharmacies in Andorra.

Where to live

  • Andorra la Vella and Escaldes-Engordany: the hospital, shops, restaurants and Caldea close by.
  • La Massana and Ordino: quieter, greener and more traditional, a few minutes' drive from the capital.
  • Sant Julià de Lòria: the lowest parish and the closest to Spain.

Property is expensive: asking prices averaged €8,027 a square metre in the second quarter of 2026, and rentals are scarce. Remember the altitude too. Even the capital is above 1,000 m, and winters are cold, though sunny. See living in Andorra and the weather by month.

This article is for orientation, not legal, tax or financial advice. Confirm every point with an Andorran lawyer, a tax adviser and the Government's immigration service before you make a decision.
