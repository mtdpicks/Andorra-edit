Andorra’s neighbours: the borders with Spain and France, and what lies just across them

By Rachel, 8 November 2026

Andorra has only two neighbours. Spain lies to the south and west, France to the north and east, and between them they wrap about 120 km of mountain border round the country: 64 km with Spain and 57 km with France. Two roads cross it. Everything else is ridges, passes and footpaths, most of it above 2,000 m.

Next to both, part of neither

People often ask whether Andorra is in Spain or France. It’s in neither. Since 1278 it has been shared by two co-princes, the Bishop of Urgell, whose cathedral is just over the Spanish border in La Seu d’Urgell, and the heir to the Counts of Foix, who today is the President of France. It has had its own written constitution since 1993, and it isn’t in the EU. It signed an association agreement with the EU in October 2026, but it will keep its own border controls. The long version is in our short history of Andorra, and the basics in where is Andorra?

The two crossings

Into Spain, the CG-1 runs south through Sant Julià de Lòria to the Runer river, with Andorran customs on one bank and the Spanish post at La Farga de Moles on the other. It’s much the busier of the two: about 7 million of the 9.6 million crossings in and out of Andorra in 2024 went this way. Into France, the CG-2 climbs to Pas de la Casa, close to 2,000 m up, and becomes the N22 just beyond the town.

Both are full frontiers, because Andorra is outside the EU and the Schengen area. Take your passport, and expect customs checks on the way out, when the shopping allowances apply (see what to buy in Andorra and visas and entry). There’s no railway and no airport inside Andorra, so every visitor arrives by one road or the other, or by helicopter from an airport with border control. In summer a rough mountain road also crosses into Spain over the Port de Cabús, unpaved on the Spanish side.

All three drive on the right and keep the same time. The two that catch visitors out are tax and phones: sales tax drops the moment you cross into Andorra, and so does your Europe roaming allowance (see phones and roaming in Andorra and Andorra’s currency).

A country with a little of each

Living between two larger neighbours shows in small ways. Andorra has no post office of its own: Spain’s Correos and France’s La Poste run side by side, each with its own Andorran stamps, and letters within the country go free. Most children are taught in the Andorran, French or Spanish school systems, which in 2026–27 have 4,334, 3,149 and 3,161 pupils. And in the streets you’ll hear Catalan, Spanish, French and Portuguese, often in the same café; see Catalan, Spanish or French?
