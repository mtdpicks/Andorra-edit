Andorra ski maps: piste maps for all three ski areas

By Rachel, 9 November 2026

Every resort in Andorra publishes its own piste map, and those are the ones to trust on the mountain. But they’re drawn like paintings, each in its own style, and it’s hard to see how the country’s three ski areas fit together. So we’ve drawn our own, from OpenStreetMap data, for Grandvalira, Pal Arinsal and Ordino Arcalís. Use the buttons above each map to pick out just the green, blue, red or black runs.

Andorra’s three ski areas at a glance

Ski areaPistesHeightWays upOfficial map
Grandvalira215 km, around 140 runs1,710 to 2,640 mSeven sectors: Encamp, Canillo, El Tarter, Soldeu, Peretol, Grau Roig and Pas de la Casagrandvalira.com
Pal ArinsalAbout 63 kmHighest lift about 2,560 mGondolas from La Massana and Arinsalpalarinsal.com
Ordino Arcalís30.5 km1,940 to 2,625 mBy road to the top of the Ordino valleyordinoarcalis.com

The three aren’t linked on skis, but one season ticket, the Andorra Pass, covers all of them, about 308 km in all. Passes and prices are in skiing in Andorra.

Grandvalira piste map

Grandvalira is the big one, and the map makes its shape clear. Most of it sits on the slopes south of the Valira d’Orient valley, above Encamp, Canillo, El Tarter and Soldeu. From there it runs east over the Envalira ridge to Grau Roig and Pas de la Casa on the French border, with Peretol, the newest sector, in between at Bordes d’Envalira. Every sector is linked on skis.

Gondolas lift you straight from the valley at Encamp, Canillo and Soldeu. The Funicamp, about 6 km long, climbs from Encamp to Solanelles, at about 2,500 m, in around 15 minutes; Canillo’s goes up to El Forn, and Soldeu’s to Espiolets. El Tarter has lifts from the village, Peretol a long chairlift from Pla de les Pedres, and at Grau Roig and Pas de la Casa you start from the car park or the village. A good day for confident skiers is the whole traverse: up from Soldeu or El Tarter, over to Grau Roig and Pas de la Casa, and back before the lifts close at 5 pm. Free buses link the sectors in the season, and the full guide is Grandvalira.

Pas de la Casa and Grau Roig piste map

From Pas de la Casa the runs spread across the big bowl above the border, and there are nursery slopes at the top end of the main street for first timers. Grandvalira’s highest lifts are up here, on the slopes below Pic Blanc, where the domain tops out at 2,640 m. Over the ridge, Grau Roig is wooded and quieter, with its own snowpark and a car park at about 2,100 m right by the lifts. In the evenings you can ski under floodlights on the Tubs slope in Pas de la Casa. More in Pas de la Casa ski holidays.

Soldeu, El Tarter and Canillo piste map

The Soldeu gondola and a six seat chairlift rise from the village to Espiolets, where the nursery area and the ski school are, and you can ski back to the village at the end of the day. El Tarter, along the valley, has nursery slopes close to the village, a snowpark and the Àliga World Cup piste. Canillo’s gondola goes up to El Forn, with easy slopes and the Mon(t) Màgic children’s circuit. At the bottom of the map you can see where the Funicamp arrives from Encamp. See Soldeu and El Tarter.

Pal Arinsal piste map

Pal Arinsal is two ski areas joined by a cable car high on the mountain. There’s no piste between them, so allow time to ride across. Pal, reached by gondola from La Massana, is wooded and sheltered, with wide green and blue runs, and it’s a great choice for first timers and stormy days. Arinsal, reached by gondola from the middle of the village, is steeper and livelier, with long blue runs and the resort’s snowpark. More in Pal Arinsal and hotels in Arinsal.

Ordino Arcalís piste map

The smallest of the three, at the head of the Ordino valley, and usually the first to open. It’s higher and wilder than the others, with 30.5 km of pistes between 1,940 and 2,625 m, and in 2026 it hosted the first FIS Freeride World Championships. The dashed lines on our map are freeride routes, which aren’t groomed like the pistes. See Ordino.

How to read an Andorran piste map

  • Colours. Green runs are the easiest, then blue, red and black. A blue in one resort can feel like a red in another, so start a grade lower on your first morning.
  • Lift names. The codes on the official maps tell you what you’re getting on. TC is a gondola, TSD a fast detachable chairlift, TSF a slower fixed chairlift and TK a drag lift. The number is the seats, so a TSD6 is a six seat chairlift.
  • Snowparks and night skiing. Grandvalira has snowparks at El Tarter, Grau Roig and Peretol, and Pal Arinsal one at Arinsal. Night skiing is on the Tubs slope in Pas de la Casa.

Our maps are drawn from OpenStreetMap, as it stood in January 2024, so a lift or a run may have changed since. They’re for planning, not for finding your way down a mountain. On the day, check the resorts’ open runs and lifts in our snow report and webcams guide, and if you’re new to skiing, read your first ski week first.
